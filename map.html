Welcome to Threading Histories

Mapping Nigerian Women's Textile Traditions, 1850–1960

This interactive map documents how British colonialism disrupted traditional textile knowledge systems across Yoruba, Igbo, and Hausa communities in Nigeria. Each marker represents a craft tradition — explore them to uncover the women, techniques, and histories behind the cloth.

How to Use This Map
1 Read the Markers

Each marker's shape indicates craft type (weaving, dyeing, embroidery, beadwork) and its color indicates status — teal for thriving, warm brown for stable, terracotta for declining.

2 Filter the Map

Use the left panel to filter by craft type or ethnolinguistic group. Uncheck any category to hide those markers and focus your exploration.

3 Explore a Location

Click any marker to open a summary popup, then select View Details to open the full side panel — with history, context, colonial impact, and sources.

4 Browse the Repository

Visit the Textile Repository to search and filter all traditions with full metadata — each entry links directly back to its map marker.
